In a flame test, the heat of the burner excites electrons in the metal ions of a compound. When the electrons fall back down, they give off light. Each metal gives the flame its own color, and a spectroscope splits that color into the metal's line spectrum.

A spectrum tube is a sealed glass tube of one gas. When electricity runs through it, the gas glows. Sending that light through a prism bends each color by a different amount, spreading the light out so you can see exactly which colors are in it.

What you see through the spectroscope

Every element has its own set of energy levels, so each one gives off its own pattern of lines. Scientists use these patterns like fingerprints to identify elements, even in stars millions of light-years away.
